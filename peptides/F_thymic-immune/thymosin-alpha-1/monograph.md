# Thymosin alpha-1

## Summary

28-residue N-acetylated thymic peptide that restores T-cell function in vitro and in vivo. Approved in many countries (not the US) as an immune-modulating adjunct, historically for hepatitis B and C. With direct-acting antivirals now standard, its role in hepatitis C has receded.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** immunomodulation — maturation and activation of dendritic and T cells; effects on regulatory T cells. Not a direct antiviral.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed in several countries (not US):** chronic hepatitis B, and as an immune adjuvant in various regimens per national labels (verify). Doses per labels.

## Important safety information and cautions

Generally well tolerated; injection-site reactions; immunomodulators warrant caution in autoimmune conditions.

## Contraindications

Per labels: hypersensitivity; immunosuppression contexts (verify).

## Side effects

Injection-site reactions, transient fever; generally mild.

## Product quality

Approved products exist outside the US; 'research' supply is unregulated.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A thymus peptide that tunes the immune system. **What do we know?** Decades of use in hepatitis B/C and immune support; hepatitis C role now historical. **What don't we know?** Modern efficacy in current treatment landscapes. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.
