# Cerebrolysin

## Summary

Standardised mixture of low-molecular-weight neurotrophic peptides derived from porcine brain tissue; no single defined sequence. Approved in many countries for stroke, traumatic brain injury and dementia; not approved by the US FDA. Cochrane evidence does not support a clear benefit in acute ischaemic stroke.

## Mechanism of action

**Proposed:** neurotrophic and neuroprotective effects attributed to peptide constituents mimicking neurotrophic factors. Because the composition is a mixture, a single molecular mechanism cannot be defined.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed in many countries (not US):** stroke, TBI, dementia per national labels (verify). Doses per local labels; no dose advice is given here.

## Important safety information and cautions

Biological product from animal tissue: theoretical transmission and immunogenicity risks; label restrictions on hypersensitivity. Cochrane authors noted trial quality limitations.

## Contraindications

Per local labels: epilepsy/seizure history, severe renal impairment, hypersensitivity (verify).

## Side effects

Generally reported as mild (dizziness, headache, injection-site reactions); rare agitation and allergic reactions reported.

## Product quality

Approved pharmaceutical products exist in many countries; the US market supply is unregulated and not interchangeable.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A pig-brain-derived peptide mixture used for brain conditions. **What do we know?** Decades of use, but the best evidence review found no clear stroke benefit. **What don't we know?** Which components work, if any. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- Cochrane found insufficient quality evidence for stroke
- Component-level pharmacology undefined
- Long-term safety data limited
