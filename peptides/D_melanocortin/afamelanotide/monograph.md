# Afamelanotide

## Summary

Synthetic alpha-MSH analogue (Nle4, D-Phe7) given as a subcutaneous implant. Approved in the EU (2014) and US (2019) to increase pain-free light exposure in adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP). The 'research' use — tanning — is off-label and outside any licence.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** melanocortin-1 receptor agonism increasing eumelanin production in skin, which reduces phototoxic light penetration in EPP. Tanning is the same pharmacology used off-label.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed:** EPP in adults; 16 mg implant every 2 months (per label; verify). Tanning use is unapproved.

## Important safety information and cautions

Implant-site reactions; increased skin pigmentation; new or changing naevi require dermatological surveillance (MC1R agonism affects melanocytes); nausea.

## Contraindications

Per label; verify. Precautionary: history of melanoma (mechanism-inferred).

## Side effects

Nausea, headache, implant reactions, hyperpigmentation; long-term melanoma risk is a monitoring consideration.

## Product quality

Approved implant exists (Scenesse). Injectable 'research' vials are unregulated and not the approved formulation.

## Handling and storage

Follow the approved label; implants require trained insertion.

## Notes for clinicians

Document any off-label tanning use and the source; advise dermatological follow-up; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** An implant that increases skin pigment, approved for a light-sensitivity disease. **What do we know?** It works for EPP. **What don't we know?** Long-term safety of off-label tanning use. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- Long-term melanoma surveillance data still accruing
- No data supporting cosmetic tanning use
