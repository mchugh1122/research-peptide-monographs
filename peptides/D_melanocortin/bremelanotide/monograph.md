# Bremelanotide (PT-141)

## Summary

Cyclic heptapeptide melanocortin-4 receptor agonist (Ac-Nle-Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys lactam). Approved in the US (2019) as Vyleesi for acquired, generalised hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women, given by subcutaneous injection as needed. The unapproved 'research' use targets male sexual function — an indication that was abandoned in development.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** melanocortin-receptor agonism (predominantly MC4R) in the central nervous system, modulating sexual desire pathways. Exact mechanism in HSDD is incompletely defined.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed (US):** premenopausal HSDD; 1.75 mg subcutaneous as needed, no more than one dose per 24 hours and eight per month. Verify against the current label.

## Important safety information and cautions

Transient blood-pressure increases; not recommended in uncontrolled hypertension or cardiovascular disease. Nausea is very common and may require antiemetics. Not for male sexual dysfunction (unapproved).

## Contraindications

Uncontrolled hypertension; known cardiovascular disease (label). Verify.

## Side effects

Nausea (most common), flushing, injection-site reactions, headache; focal hyperpigmentation reported.

## Product quality

Approved product exists (Vyleesi). 'Research' powder supply is unregulated and not the approved product.

## Handling and storage

Follow the approved label; no advice for unapproved supply.

## Notes for clinicians

Off-label male use should be documented: dose, frequency and blood-pressure effects; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** An injection approved for low sexual desire in premenopausal women. **What do we know?** Modest, real benefits in trials, with prominent nausea. **What don't we know?** Long-term effects; benefit in men is unproven. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it, the dose, and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- Male sexual dysfunction indication abandoned; data unpublished
- Long-term cardiovascular effects of intermittent use not studied
