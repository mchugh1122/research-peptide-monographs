# Gonadorelin

## Summary

Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH): pyroGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-Gly-Leu-Arg-Pro-Gly-NH2, the hypothalamic decapeptide whose structure was solved in 1971 — the foundation of all GnRH agonist/antagonist medicines. Approved products exist for diagnostic testing and infertility treatment (pulsatile administration).

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** pituitary GnRH-receptor agonism releasing LH and FSH; pulsatile delivery is essential — continuous exposure suppresses the axis (the basis of GnRH-agonist therapy).

## Indications and dose

**Licensed:** diagnostic testing of pituitary function; induction of ovulation via pulsatile pump (per approved labels; verify). Doses per label.

## Important safety information and cautions

Injection-site reactions; ovarian hyperstimulation risk in fertility use (label). Off-label continuous-dosing misuse produces axis suppression rather than stimulation.

## Contraindications

Per label: pregnancy, hormone-sensitive cancers (verify).

## Side effects

Injection-site reactions, headache, nausea; with pulsatile fertility use, abdominal discomfort.

## Product quality

Approved products exist; 'research' supply is unregulated.

## Handling and storage

Follow approved labels.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** The master reproductive hormone, used medically for testing and fertility. **What do we know?** Decades of established use. **What don't we know?** Nothing major — it is a well-characterised medicine. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.
