# Oxytocin

## Summary

Nonapeptide hormone (Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly, cyclic disulfide) first synthesised in 1953 (Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1955). Among the most widely used medicines in obstetrics (labour induction/augmentation and postpartum haemorrhage). 'Research' supply for alleged social/behavioural effects is unapproved use.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** oxytocin-receptor agonism — uterine contraction, milk ejection, and central roles in social behaviour that are the subject of ongoing research.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed:** induction/augmentation of labour, postpartum haemorrhage, incomplete abortion (per labels; WHO essential medicine). Intranasal behavioural uses are investigational. Doses per label.

## Important safety information and cautions

Uterine hyperstimulation; water retention/hyponatraemia with high infusion rates; hypotension with rapid IV administration.

## Contraindications

Per label (e.g., significant cephalopelvic disproportion, fetal distress where delivery is not imminent); verify.

## Side effects

Nausea, vomiting; dose-related water intoxication in obstetric infusion; transient hypotension/tachycardia on bolus.

## Product quality

Approved products exist worldwide; 'research' vials are unregulated.

## Handling and storage

Follow approved labels; refrigerated storage per product.

## Regulatory and sport status

| Jurisdiction | Status |

| Sport | Not prohibited by WADA to current knowledge (verify). |

## Notes for clinicians

Ask about source and route when non-obstetric use is reported; document and report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** The hormone behind childbirth and bonding — a mainstream medicine. **What do we know?** Extremely well characterised in obstetric use. **What don't we know?** Whether unapproved intranasal 'bonding' uses have reliable effects. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- Behavioural/intranasal uses lack regulatory-grade evidence
