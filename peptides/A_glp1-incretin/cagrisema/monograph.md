# CagriSema

## Summary

Fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide 2.4 mg (long-acting amylin analogue) and semaglutide 2.4 mg (GLP-1 receptor agonist) in a single once-weekly subcutaneous injection. Phase 3 (REDEFINE) results showed mean weight reductions among the largest reported for an injectable therapy. Under regulatory review; an FDA decision has been anticipated around December 2026.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** two complementary satiety pathways — GLP-1 receptor agonism (semaglutide) and amylin-receptor agonism (cagrilintide, with the fatty-acid modification conferring once-weekly dosing). Each component independently reduces appetite; the combination is additive or more than additive in trials.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications yet (under review).** Trial dose: 2.4 mg cagrilintide + 2.4 mg semaglutide once weekly.

## Important safety information and cautions

Trial-reported adverse events were mainly gastrointestinal and generally mild–moderate; discontinuations were higher than placebo. Class considerations from both components apply: thyroid C-cell tumour warning (semaglutide), pancreatitis, gallbladder disease. Final safety positioning awaits labelling.

## Contraindications

None formally established until approved. Class-inferred precautions: personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2; pregnancy.

## Side effects

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation; injection-site reactions.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A combination of two appetite-regulating medicines in one weekly injection. **What do we know?** Large trials show substantial weight loss. **What don’t we know?** Long-term safety; it is not yet approved. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you are using it and where it came from.
