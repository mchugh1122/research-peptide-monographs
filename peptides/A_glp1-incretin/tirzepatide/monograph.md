# Tirzepatide

## Summary

Once-weekly dual agonist of the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP-1 receptors: a 39-residue peptide with Aib at positions 2 and 13 and a C20 fatty-diacid side chain on Lys20. Approved in the US for type 2 diabetes (2022) and chronic weight management (2023); cardiovascular outcomes trials are ongoing.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonism; glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, delayed gastric emptying and reduced appetite. Whether GIP-receptor agonism adds to GLP-1-mediated weight loss beyond receptor occupancy is debated; the clinical weight-loss effect is larger than for GLP-1 monoagonism at approved doses.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed indications (US):** type 2 diabetes and chronic weight management; 2.5 mg starting dose, maintenance 5–15 mg once weekly subcutaneous. Verify against the current label.

## Important safety information and cautions

US boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours (rodent findings). Warnings include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, acute kidney injury, severe gastrointestinal disease (gastroparesis reported), and diabetic retinopathy complications. Hypoglycaemia mainly with insulin or secretagogues.

## Contraindications

Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; MEN2; hypersensitivity. Stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy.

## Side effects

Common: nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation, decreased appetite; mostly mild–moderate, dose-escalation related.

## Notes for clinicians

For unapproved use, document source, dose and duration; screen for gastrointestinal, biliary and thyroid-related symptoms; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A weekly injection that activates two gut-hormone pathways to lower glucose and appetite. **What do we know?** Strong trial evidence for glycaemic control and substantial weight loss. **What don’t we know?** Cardiovascular-outcome results are pending; very long-term safety is not yet described. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you are taking it, the dose and the source.

## Evidence gaps

- Cardiovascular outcomes trials not yet reported
- Long-term (>3 year) data limited
- Independent analysis of non-approved market supply not yet entered
