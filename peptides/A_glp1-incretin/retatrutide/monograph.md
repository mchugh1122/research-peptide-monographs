# Retatrutide

## Summary

Investigational once-weekly triple agonist of the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors; a 39-residue peptide with Aib and a fatty-diacid side chain (sequence to verify). Phase 2 results showed the largest weight reductions reported to date for a peptide in obesity; phase 3 trials are ongoing. Not approved anywhere at the time of writing, yet unapproved supply is widely sold.

## Mechanism of action

**Established (target pharmacology):** triple agonism — GLP-1 and GIP effects on appetite and insulin secretion, plus glucagon-receptor agonism proposed to increase energy expenditure and hepatic fat mobilisation. Human weight-loss mechanism attribution is from secondary analyses; treat as proposed.

## Evidence summary

| Tier | Finding | Ref. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Phase 2 obesity: −24.2% body weight at 48 weeks (12 mg group) | [10.1056/NEJMoa2301972](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2301972) |

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications. No established dose.** Doses below are study conditions, not recommendations. Phase 2 tested 1, 4, 8 and 12 mg once weekly.

## Important safety information and cautions

Safety profile in phase 2 resembled other incretin agents — mainly gastrointestinal events, including dose-escalation-related nausea. No boxed warning can exist for an unapproved product; class concerns (thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease) remain to be defined in phase 3.

## Contraindications

None formally established. Mechanism-inferred precautions apply to pregnancy and to a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (class effect).

## Side effects

Trial-reported: nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation; cardiac arrhythmia events were numerically higher in one dose group and require phase 3 scrutiny (to be updated with published data).

## Product quality

No approved product exists. Any purchased supply is unregulated; quality, identity and sterility are unknown. Independent analytical studies to be entered when available.

## Handling and storage

No validated public data. Reconstitution instructions are deliberately not given for an unapproved compound.

## Regulatory and sport status

| Jurisdiction | Status |

## Notes for clinicians

If a patient reports using retatrutide, treat it as an undocumented, unregulated product: document source, dose and duration; consider cardiac monitoring given the phase 2 arrhythmia signal; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** An experimental weekly injection aimed at three hormone targets. **What do we know?** One phase 2 trial showed very large weight loss; it is not approved and phase 3 is not finished. **What don’t we know?** Safety, long-term effects, and whether the weight loss translates to health benefits. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you are using it, the dose, and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- Phase 3 efficacy and safety not yet published
- Cardiovascular and long-term safety unknown
- Independent analysis of the unregulated supply not yet entered
