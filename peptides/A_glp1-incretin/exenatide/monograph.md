# Exenatide

## Summary

Synthetic form of exendin-4, a 39-residue peptide isolated from the venom of the Gila monster (Heloderma suspectum); it shares ~53% sequence identity with human GLP-1 and resists DPP-4 degradation. The first GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for type 2 diabetes (twice-daily injection, 2005); an extended-release once-weekly form followed. Discovery (1992) was basic venom biochemistry that became the template for the entire incretin class.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** GLP-1 receptor agonism — glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, slowed gastric emptying and reduced appetite. Native exendin-4’s resistance to enzymatic degradation was the key early insight that made therapeutic use practical.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed indications (US):** type 2 diabetes; 5 or 10 µg twice daily (immediate release) or 2 mg once weekly (extended release). Verify against the current label.

## Important safety information and cautions

US boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours (rodents). Warnings include acute pancreatitis (post-marketing reports), gallbladder disease, and renal impairment in the setting of dehydration. Nausea is prominent early and is the main reason for discontinuation.

## Contraindications

Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; MEN2; hypersensitivity.

## Side effects

Common: nausea (dose-limiting), vomiting, diarrhoea; injection-site nodules with the once-weekly form; weight loss; headache.

## Notes for clinicians

Exenatide remains a useful comparator and historical anchor: it established the GLP-1 class. For any patient using an unapproved supply, document source, dose and symptoms, and report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A diabetes medicine copied from a lizard-venom hormone; injected twice daily or weekly. **What do we know?** It improves glucose control and modestly reduces weight; decades of use. **What don’t we know?** How it compares in long-term outcomes with newer agents — it has largely been superseded. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you take it and the dose.

## Evidence gaps
