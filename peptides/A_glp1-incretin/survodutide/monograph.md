# Survodutide

## Summary

Investigational once-weekly dual agonist of the glucagon and GLP-1 receptors (BI 456906): an oxyntomodulin-derived 29-residue backbone with Aib2 and a fatty-acid-modified Lys (sequence to verify). Phase 2 studies support weight loss and metabolic-dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) endpoints; phase 3 (SYNCHRONIZE) trials are ongoing/reported.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** GLP-1 receptor agonism (appetite, glucose) combined with glucagon-receptor agonism proposed to raise energy expenditure and mobilise hepatic fat. Liver-fat and fibrosis effects are trial outcomes; the mechanistic split between weight loss and direct hepatic action is not fully resolved.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications. No established dose.** Phase 2 tested 0.6–4.8 mg once weekly (46 weeks, 20-week escalation); phase 3 uses 3.6 and 6.0 mg.

## Important safety information and cautions

Gastrointestinal events dominate (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea), mostly mild–moderate and dose-escalation related. Glucagon agonism warrants monitoring of glycaemia in diabetes and heart-rate effects; phase 3 data will define the profile.

## Contraindications

None formally established. Pregnancy safety unknown; class precautions apply.

## Side effects

Nausea (most common), vomiting, diarrhoea, decreased appetite.

## Product quality

No approved product exists; unregulated supply should be treated as unknown quality.

## Handling and storage

No validated public data; instructions deliberately not given.

## Notes for clinicians

If use is reported, document source and dose; monitor glucose (especially with diabetes medicines), heart rate and GI symptoms; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** An experimental weekly injection acting on two hormones, including one that may burn energy and reduce liver fat. **What do we know?** Phase 2 trials show weight loss and liver-fat reduction; it is not approved. **What don’t we know?** Long-term safety and phase 3 outcomes. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.
