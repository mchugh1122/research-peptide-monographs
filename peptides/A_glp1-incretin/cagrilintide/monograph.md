# Cagrilintide

## Summary

Long-acting amylin analogue: a 37-residue peptide based on amylin with a Cys2–Cys7 disulfide bridge and a hexadecanedioic (C16 diacid) side chain on Lys1, giving once-weekly pharmacokinetics. Acts at amylin/calcitonin receptors to suppress appetite. Studied alone and as the fixed-dose combination CagriSema; also sold alone as a ‘research’ peptide.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** amylin-receptor agonism; central reduction of food intake (satiety), slowing of gastric emptying and suppression of glucagon in some settings. Exact brain-region contributions are under investigation.

## Evidence summary

| Tier | Finding | Ref. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Phase 2: dose-dependent weight loss up to −10.8% at 26 weeks (4.5 mg) | [10.1016/S0140-6736(21)01751-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)01751-7) |

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications as monotherapy. No established dose.** Trial doses ranged 0.3–4.5 mg once weekly; combination use is 2.4 mg.

## Important safety information and cautions

Mainly gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, constipation, diarrhoea). Hypoglycaemia is relevant in diabetes when combined with insulin or secretagogues. Long-term safety, including pancreatic and cardiac effects, is not yet established.

## Contraindications

None formally established. Pregnancy safety unknown.

## Side effects

Nausea (most common), constipation, diarrhoea; injection-site reactions.

## Product quality

No approved monotherapy product exists. ‘Research’ supply is unregulated; identity, purity and sterility unknown.

## Handling and storage

No validated public data; instructions deliberately not given.

## Notes for clinicians

Document source, dose and symptoms if use is reported; GI tolerability is the main practical issue; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A lab-made version of the hormone amylin that reduces appetite. **What do we know?** One phase 2 trial showed dose-related weight loss; it is not approved alone. **What don’t we know?** Long-term safety. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- No phase 3 monotherapy programme published
- Long-term and cardiovascular safety unknown
- Independent analysis of the unregulated supply
