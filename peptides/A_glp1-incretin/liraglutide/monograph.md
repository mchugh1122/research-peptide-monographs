# Liraglutide

## Summary

GLP-1 receptor agonist with 97% homology to native GLP-1. The substitution Lys34→Arg and a C16 (palmitoyl) fatty-acid side chain on Lys20 confer albumin binding and a ~13-hour half-life, allowing once-daily dosing. Approved for type 2 diabetes (2010) and weight management at 3.0 mg (2014). US generics of the diabetes dose are now marketed.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** GLP-1 receptor agonism — glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, delayed gastric emptying, and reduced appetite and energy intake via central pathways.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed indications (US):** type 2 diabetes (0.6–1.8 mg daily subcutaneous); chronic weight management at 3.0 mg daily. Verify against the current label.

## Important safety information and cautions

US boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours (rodents). Warnings include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, acute kidney injury, and suicidality monitoring (post-marketing reports for weight-management dose). Hypoglycaemia mainly with insulin or secretagogues.

## Contraindications

Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; MEN2; hypersensitivity. Stop before planned pregnancy (safety data lacking).

## Side effects

Common: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation; injection-site reactions; increased heart rate (2–3 bpm mean).

## Notes for clinicians

Ask about source, dose and duration for unapproved use; monitor for gastrointestinal, biliary and thyroid-related symptoms; report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A daily injection that mimics a gut hormone. **What do we know?** Reduces glucose, aids weight loss, and lowered cardiovascular events in one large trial. **What don’t we know?** Very long-term safety; effects beyond the trial durations. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you take it, the dose and the source.

## Evidence gaps

- Long-term (>5 year) data limited
- Independent analysis of compounded/non-approved supply not yet entered
