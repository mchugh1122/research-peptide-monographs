# Semaglutide

## Summary

Long-acting glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist with 94% sequence homology to native GLP-1. Substitutions (Aib at position 2) and a fatty-diacid side chain on Lys20 confer resistance to DPP-4 degradation and reversible albumin binding, giving a half-life of about one week. Approved for type 2 diabetes (injectable 2017, oral 2019) and chronic weight management (2.4 mg once weekly, 2021) in the US; cardiovascular risk reduction in people with established cardiovascular disease and obesity is a labelled indication following SELECT.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** agonism of the GLP-1 receptor. Glucose-dependent insulin secretion and glucagon suppression, slowed gastric emptying, and reduced appetite and energy intake through central pathways. Human pharmacokinetics: half-life about one week; absorption of the oral form requires the SNAC absorption enhancer.

## Indications and dose

**Licensed indications (US):** type 2 diabetes (0.5–2 mg once weekly subcutaneous; 3, 7 or 14 mg once daily oral); chronic weight management at 2.4 mg once weekly; reduction of cardiovascular risk in adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity. Doses are label doses for approved products; verify against the current label.

## Important safety information and cautions

US boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumours (medullary thyroid carcinoma) in rodents; relevance in humans undetermined. Warnings include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, acute kidney injury in the setting of dehydration, and diabetic retinopathy complications (signal in SUSTAIN-6). Hypoglycaemia risk mainly with insulin or sulfonylureas.

## Contraindications

Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2; hypersensitivity. Not for type 1 diabetes. Stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy (pregnancy safety data lacking).

## Side effects

Common (trial-derived, dose-dependent): nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain. Discontinuation driven mainly by gastrointestinal effects.

## Product quality

To be added from independent analytical studies. Compounded and ‘research’ supplies of semaglutide salts are reported to exist and are not interchangeable with approved products; this needs primary-source verification.

## Handling and storage

Follow the approved product label. No advice is given for unapproved supplies.

## Notes for clinicians

Ask about source and form when patients report using unapproved ‘research’ semaglutide: dose, titration, injection practice and any gastrointestinal or gallbladder symptoms. Document and report adverse events.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A weekly injection (or daily tablet) that mimics a gut hormone to lower glucose and reduce appetite. **What do we know?** Large trials show meaningful weight loss and fewer cardiovascular events in selected groups. **What don’t we know?** Long-term effects beyond ~4 years, and safety in pregnancy. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you are taking it, the dose, and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- No long-term (>5 year) efficacy or safety data yet published
- Limited evidence in older adults, severe renal/hepatic impairment subgroups for the obesity indication
- Independent analysis of non-approved market supply not yet entered
