# Dermorphin

## Summary

Heptapeptide opioid (Tyr-D-Ala-Phe-Gly-Tyr-Pro-Ser-NH2) from South American frog skin, with potent mu-opioid activity. No medical use; prohibited in sport and historically used in equine doping ('frog juice'). Its D-amino acid confers resistance to enzymatic degradation.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** high-affinity mu-opioid receptor agonism — essentially an endogenous opioid.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications.** Any use is illicit/unapproved; no dose is endorsed.

## Important safety information and cautions

Opioid: respiratory depression, dependence, withdrawal; potency exceeds morphine on a weight basis in some assays. Unregulated supply is a poisoning risk.

## Contraindications

Opioid contraindications apply by mechanism (respiratory disease, concurrent CNS depressants).

## Side effects

Opioid effects: sedation, respiratory depression, nausea, dependence.

## Product quality

Illicit supply; purity unknown; represents a real toxicological risk.

## Handling and storage

Instructions deliberately not given.

## Notes for clinicians

Treat exposure as opioid exposure: consider naloxone where indicated; report to poison/toxicology services.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A frog-skin opioid, sometimes called 'frog juice'. **What do we know?** It is a potent opioid with all the dangers that implies. **What don't we know?** Nothing relevant — it is dangerous and banned. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** Immediately, if you have used it.
