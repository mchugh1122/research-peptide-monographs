# BPC-157

## Summary

Synthetic 15-residue peptide (Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val) described as the active fragment of BPC, a protein reported in gastric juice. First described in 1993. Not approved anywhere; absent from all major pharmacopoeias. The July 2026 FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee reviewed it for the 503A bulks list; FDA staff proposed against inclusion while the committee narrowly recommended it — final FDA action is pending.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** none identified.

**Proposed (animal or in vitro only):** endothelial protection, tissue repair and anti-inflammatory effects, derived almost entirely from one research group's animal studies. Human pharmacokinetics not identified.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications. No established dose.** Human administrations documented in the FDA briefing (study conditions only): up to 2 mg/kg rectal enema daily × 8 days; 80 mg enema daily × 2 weeks (UC); 2–4 mg intra-articular (knee pain); 10 mg intravesical (interstitial cystitis); 10–20 mg IV (healthy subjects). These are not recommendations.

## Important safety information and cautions

FDA's July 2026 evaluation states the substance is **not well characterized**: inconsistent naming (multiple salts sold under one name), lack of impurity, aggregate, bioburden and endotoxin data, and **possible immunogenicity**, especially for injectable and nasal routes. Repeat-dose (28-day) animal studies via intramuscular injection showed **clinically relevant safety signals potentially related to coagulopathy and the liver**. Longer-term toxicity studies are unavailable.

## Contraindications

None established, because the compound has not been formally evaluated.

**Precautionary considerations (mechanism-inferred, unverified):** active cancer, given proposed effects on blood-vessel growth; pregnancy and breastfeeding, because there are no safety data.

## Side effects

Frequency not known. In the small human studies, headache and flatulence were most commonly reported (enema route). Three FAERS reports describe injection-site reactions, dyspnoea and (with TB-500 co-use) hyperpigmentation; attribution unclear. Animal toxicology signals as above.

## Product quality

FDA: no USP/NF monograph; certificates of analysis lack impurity detail; no microbial bioburden or endotoxin testing identified. Vendor certificates of analysis are never used as evidence here.

## Handling and storage

No validated stability data entered. Reconstitution and administration instructions are deliberately not given for an unapproved compound.

## Notes for clinicians

Useful questions: where it was obtained and in what form, the dose and duration used, other medicines and supplements, and the reason for use. Document the answers, consider the safety section above (immunogenicity, coagulation/liver signals from animal data), and report any adverse event to the national scheme.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A lab-made chain of 15 amino acids. **What do we know?** Mostly animal studies from one research group. It is not an approved medicine. **What don't we know?** Whether it works in people, what dose is safe, and what is actually in products sold online — FDA found the substance poorly characterized. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you are using or considering it, where it came from, and how much you take.

## Evidence gaps

- No published controlled human trials (one UC trial exists only as a meeting abstract)
- No human pharmacokinetic or long-term safety data
- Animal literature dominated by a single research group; independent replication needed
- No independent analysis of marketed products yet entered
