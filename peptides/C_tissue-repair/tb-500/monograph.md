# TB-500

## Summary

Synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4, residues 17–23 (Lys-Lys-Thr-Glu-Thr-Gln), typically as the N-terminal acetylated peptide — the form characterised analytically as TB-500. Marketed as a tissue-repair and recovery agent for horses and humans; not approved anywhere as a medicine. The parent protein, thymosin beta-4, is an actin-regulating peptide with a body of animal repair literature.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** none in humans. **Proposed (animal):** promotion of cell migration, angiogenesis and tissue repair, attributed to the parent protein's actin-binding and integrin-linked kinase activation; the 7-residue fragment's own pharmacology is poorly defined.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications. No established dose.** No human efficacy trials identified for the fragment itself.

## Important safety information and cautions

No human safety database. Injectable peptide products carry immunogenicity risk; unregulated supply adds identity, purity and sterility unknowns.

## Contraindications

None established. Precautionary: active cancer (angiogenesis-promoting peptides), pregnancy.

## Side effects

Unknown in humans; injection-site reactions reported anecdotally.

## Product quality

No approved product; no pharmacopoeial monograph. Independent analyses of market supply to be entered.

## Handling and storage

Instructions deliberately not given for an unapproved compound.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A 7-amino-acid fragment of a repair protein, sold for injury recovery. **What do we know?** Animal data on the parent protein; nothing clinical on the fragment. **What don't we know?** Whether it does anything in humans, and its safety. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it and where it came from.
