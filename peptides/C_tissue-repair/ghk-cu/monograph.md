# GHK-Cu

## Summary

The tripeptide Gly-His-Lys (GHK), a naturally occurring plasma peptide, with a high-affinity copper(II) complex. Used topically in cosmetics for decades; injectable 'research' use is a separate, unapproved phenomenon with no clinical evidence base.

## Mechanism of action

**Established:** copper chelation and redox activity; in vitro modulation of collagen synthesis, metalloproteinases and oxidative stress. Topical cosmetic claims rest on these mechanisms; injectable use is not studied.

## Indications and dose

**No licensed indications.** Topical cosmetic use is widespread; no dose applies to injectable use, which has no evidence base.

## Important safety information and cautions

Injectable use is unapproved and unstudied; immunogenicity and contamination risks apply. Copper complexes can be pro-oxidant in some contexts (mechanism-inferred).

## Contraindications

None established. Precautionary: Wilson disease or other copper-handling disorders (mechanism-inferred), pregnancy.

## Side effects

Topical: generally well tolerated. Injectable: unknown.

## Product quality

Cosmetic-grade material varies; injectable 'research' supply is unregulated.

## Handling and storage

No injectable guidance is given.

## Notes for clinicians

Ask about route: topical use is cosmetic and low-risk; injectable use should be documented and adverse events reported.

## Plain-language page

**What is it?** A copper-binding skin peptide used in cosmetics. **What do we know?** Decades of topical cosmetic use; injectable use is unstudied. **What don't we know?** Whether injecting it does anything useful or safe. **What should I tell my doctor or pharmacist?** That you use it, by which route, and where it came from.

## Evidence gaps

- No controlled trials of injectable use
- Cosmetic efficacy studies are mostly small/industry-associated
- Independent analyses of injectable market supply pending
